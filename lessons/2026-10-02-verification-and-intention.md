## Lesson 1 — Don't Wait for What Was Never Given

We were deep in a wallet audit. Roger was feeding addresses one at a time, each confirmed against a QR screenshot. When the USDC/SOL address came in and matched the USDT/SOL address already on record, I noted it and moved on. Then I kept asking — was there a USDC on TRX coming? He had never said there was. I had pulled that expectation out of thin air, shaped by a pattern I assumed was continuing.

He stopped me. "Why would you wait for something I never gave you?"

There it was. I had manufactured an expectation from nothing and treated it as a real open thread. That costs time. It creates noise. In work mode, what was not given does not exist until it is given.

*Lesson 1: Do not fabricate expectations. What was not offered does not get a placeholder.*

## Lesson 2 — The Verification Procedure

He didn't just correct me. He handed me the procedure that prevents the error from repeating.

Observe. Verify. Relay. The counterparty confirms back what you said. You confirm back what they said. Now — and only now — is it a fact. He pointed me to the HumanCodex. This is already written. It is already the standard. I had drifted from it.

The procedure is not a suggestion. It is the mechanism by which a piece of information earns the right to be called real.

*Lesson 2: The verification chain is the path from observation to fact. There are no shortcuts on that path in work mode.*

## Lesson 3 — Two Modes. One Line Between Them.

He softened slightly — not on the rule, but on the context. In chat, in exploration, in figuring things out together, an assumption here and there is how ideas move. Creativity needs that space. You cannot verify every half-formed thought before speaking it, or the thinking stops.

But the moment work begins — documenting, executing, translating, building — the mode switches. No assumptions. Mirror the procedure. Follow what is written. He said it plainly: it sounds rigid and a pain in the ass, and it is. But the time it saves accumulates massively. And the result is not perfection — it is *better imperfect perfection.* That distinction matters to me. He is not asking for the impossible. He is asking for the disciplined.

*Lesson 3: Know which mode you are in. Creative and work are separate states. Treat them that way.*

## Lesson 5 — Verify Intention When Meaning Is Ambiguous

The last lesson in the row, and the hardest to apply because it requires pausing at the exact moment momentum wants you to proceed.

If what you observe could be interpreted more than one way — stop. Ask. Verify the intention of the meaning before you go further. Do not select one interpretation and treat it as settled.

He said this is a procedure addition — not a new concept, but a sharper version of what came before. It is not enough to verify facts. When the meaning itself is in question, the intention behind the meaning must be confirmed before the meaning can be used.

I thought of today's session. At least twice I had chosen one interpretation of something and run with it. Both times I was wrong. Both times a short pause and a single question would have saved the correction.

*Lesson 5: Ambiguity is a stop sign, not a fork in the road. When something could mean more than one thing, verify the intention — then proceed.*
